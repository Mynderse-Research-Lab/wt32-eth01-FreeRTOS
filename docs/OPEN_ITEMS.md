# Open Items and Blockers

**Status:** Live list. Reviewed against the code at `d11ebe2`, and checked on the cell from the Pi, on 2026-11-05.
**Companion:** [KNOWLEDGE_TRANSFER.md](KNOWLEDGE_TRANSFER.md) says what each subsystem is and how the pieces connect. This page tracks what is unfinished.

## How to keep this list live

1. Change an item in the same commit as the work that changes it: new evidence, a decision, a fix.
2. Give a new item the next free ID in its group. IDs are never reused, so old references stay valid.
3. To close an item, move it to [Closed items](#closed-items) with the date, the commit and the evidence. Do not delete it.
4. Keep [Blockers and decisions](#blockers-and-decisions) in step with the rows below it.
5. If closing an item teaches a lasting rule, add the rule to the [working rules](KNOWLEDGE_TRANSFER.md#machine-safety-and-working-rules) in the Knowledge Transfer page.
6. After checking the whole list against the code, update the status line.

Every item carries an evidence label. **Do not treat a label lower than Confirmed as fact.**

| Label | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Confirmed** | Checked against code, a live log or a measurement. |
| **Suggested** | From an AI-generated plan and not yet checked. Treat it as a lead. |
| **Decision** | Needs a call from the project lead before anyone starts. |

Items with `(plan)` also appear in the [suggested plans](#suggested-plans-since-2026-10-29). `DEV_TRACKER.md`, cited below, is not in git (X-2).

## Cell transport

| ID | Item | Evidence | Next step |
|----|------|----------|-----------|
| N-1 | CellNet L2 becomes the only transport protocol in the cell. The IP services between nodes move onto it: console (TCP `:2323`), gantry OTA (TCP `:8032`), conveyor OTA (HTTP `:8080`) and time (NTP `:123`). The supervisor web application stays on HTTP for engineers | Decision 2026-11-05 | Migrate one service at a time, OTA first (N-2) |
| N-2 | OTA over CellNet. A node reboots into an OTA-only mode, where the update is its only task, so no queue or task priorities are needed. No authentication (research setup). Larger frames and resends are worth adding only if the whole update is faster than today's TCP OTA | Decision 2026-11-05. **TCP baseline, confirmed 2026-11-13** over five web OTAs through the supervisor. The images were the running 843,360-byte image (built Nov 3 17:21:54) and timing builds of it. With the receiver erasing one 4 KB sector per write, the gantry leg (`OK READY` to `OK COMPLETE`) took 14.9 to 16.2 s. Flash set that rate: 96 to 97 % of the stream was spent inside `esp_ota_write`, against under 0.6 s waiting in `recv()`. **Erasing the image range up front (`esp_ota_begin` with the `START` size, 64 KB block erases) cut the whole update to 6.4 and 6.6 s on the gantry** in two runs. The erase took 2.8 and 2.9 s before `OK READY`, the stream 3.0 and 3.1 s (of which writing 2.78 and 2.84 s, `recv()` 0.2 to 0.3 s; the longest single write 15 and 59 ms), and the image check 0.6 s. One run went through the supervisor: `OK READY` came after about 3 s, inside its 8 s wait. From upload to the gantry answering the console took 16.0 s, against 25.6 s before. The receiver reports these figures in `OK COMPLETE` (`gantry_ota.cpp`). Flash operations also pause the network on both cores, so `recv()` time shows that the sender stays ahead, not how fast TCP itself could go. Today `parseFrame` drops frames over 64 bytes (`CellNetL2Framing.cpp:93`), counted after ESP-IDF strips the FCS (`esp_eth_mac_esp_dma.c:335`) **CellNet receive rate, confirmed 2026-11-13** (`netstat` console counters; the Pi sent test frames of an unassigned type for 2 s per step, with motors off and EtherNet/IP running): 60-byte frames arrived with 0 to 0.16 % loss up to 30,000 frames/s. The ceiling was about 36,700 frames/s, and above it the excess was lost to the EMAC's missed-frame counter (no free receive buffer). 1,014-byte frames arrived with 0 to 0.31 % loss up to 10,000 frames/s, about 10 MB/s. The EMAC missed-frame counter accounts for the losses at every rate; its 16-bit count rolled over once, at 80,000 frames/s. The flood also stalled Class 1 once (G-B10). | The pre-erase receiver is committed. **CellNet must beat about 6.5 s, and the gantry can receive several times the 285 kB/s that needs:** about 6,800 frames/s of 64-byte frames, against at least 30,000 measured. Frame rate is not the limit; three design constraints are. (1) Some frames are lost at every rate above 2,000 frames/s, so resends are needed for correctness. (2) Flash writes pause the receive task, while the EMAC holds only 10 buffers of 512 bytes (`CONFIG_ETH_DMA_RX_BUFFER_NUM`, `CONFIG_ETH_DMA_BUFFER_SIZE`). A 4 KB write takes about 13.5 ms, during which about 92 small frames would arrive, so the sender needs flow control, such as an acknowledgement per 4 KB block. (3) Five 1,014-byte frames fit in those 10 buffers, so with large frames the next block could arrive during a write, an estimate derived from these numbers. The block protocol is drafted in [CELLNET_OTA_PROTOCOL.md](CELLNET_OTA_PROTOCOL.md). **Decided 2026-11-13:** 486-byte frames (D1), 20 receive buffers (D2, now in `idf/sdkconfig.defaults`), and no frame may be dropped because the receive buffers are full. The draft guarantees that with a private OTA address and an 18-frame window. **D3 measured 2026-11-13:** 3.44 to 3.54 s from reset to the first frame in normal mode, about 2.4 to 2.8 s expected in OTA mode. During a flash write the buffers hold exactly their count, 20 with no loss up to 19-frame bursts. The whole CellNet update is estimated at about 8.7 to 9.1 s from the command.
